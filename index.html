Short videos about obscure indie games that people who played them actually liked. Every game here is on Steam, credited to its developer and linked below.

  • Rogue Party

    Chris Antoni · 2018-10-31

    Rogue Party throws one to four rogues into the randomized Dungeon of Doom, where chests, enemies, and bosses shift every run. It's FPS-style dungeon shooting layered with roguelike loot, potions, and drop-in drop-out multiplayer. Reviewers admit it's rough around the edges but say it shines in co-op, packed with strategy, laughs, and an active developer still adding content. If you love messy, chaotic dungeon runs with friends, this small-budget crawler delivers exactly that.

  • Chronescher

    PurpleSloth · 2022-08-31

    Chronescher drops you into an Escher-esque world where geometry bends and nothing stays where you left it. Place portals, snapshot the level with time anchors, and shift perspective to uncover paths that shouldn't exist. Spread across six biomes, each introducing new mechanics, it builds toward a quiet mystery about who you're actually playing. Reviewers compare it favorably to Monument Valley and Manifold Garden, calling out the low price for the amount of content. With only 58 reviews, it's a puzzle game that hasn't found its audience yet.

  • REVISITOR

    Lead Games · 2021-11-19

    Revisitor is a first-person puzzle-platformer built by a solo developer, set inside a simulated 'Revisitor program.' You navigate with two teleport types, Return and Blink, through increasingly tricky missions filled with hidden logs and challenge objectives. Reviewers compare it favorably to Portal 2, praising its smooth movement and clever mechanics. Best of all, it's completely free, with players reporting eight to fifteen hours of content to fully complete.

  • SokoWinter

    Gray Boss · 2022-03-13

    SokoWinter takes the classic sokoban formula and adds one sharp twist: ice. Push blocks across slippery tiles where everything keeps sliding until it hits something solid, and suddenly every puzzle demands new thinking. With 100 handcrafted levels, four unlockable penguins, an undo button, and a minimalist winter aesthetic, it's the kind of low-pressure puzzle game that rewards patience without ever feeling punishing.
