Short videos about obscure indie games that people who played them actually liked. Every game here is on Steam, credited to its developer and linked below.

  • Tales of the Tiny Planet

    Pixelsplit · 2017-08-03

    Tales of the Tiny Planet is a physics-puzzle adventure controlled entirely with one button. You guide small rolling planets through six worlds and seventy-two levels, timing precise presses to steer them through portals. It starts calm and cute, then turns genuinely difficult, with players citing worlds five and six as brutal tests of timing. With local co-op, a leaderboard, and a soundtrack players keep praising, it's a small, tightly built game that slipped past almost everyone.

  • Sacrificial Lighthouse

    PhobiaGamesNet · 2021-10-28

    Sacrificial Lighthouse follows Maya, stranded on a lighthouse island during a storm, in a short hand-drawn adventure built around atmosphere and choice. Reviewers praise the haunting art style, eerie musical score, and a branched ending that reframes the whole story. It's light on puzzles and heavy on mood, closer to a walking sim than a traditional adventure game. At under an hour long, it's a quiet, sad experience for fans of story-first games like What Remains of Edith Finch.

  • DYSTORIA

    Tri-Coastal Games · 2017-02-20

    Dystoria is a six-axis arcade shooter that lets your ship cling to any surface in the level, floors, walls, ceilings, all of it. Reviewers have compared its maze-like, wall-crawling combat to Descent, but wrapped in a pulsing synthwave, 1984-inspired aesthetic. Blast insectoid enemies, collect orbs, upgrade your ship across thirty levels of increasingly twisted space labyrinths. With a 94% positive rating from players, it's a puzzle-shooter hybrid built for anyone who misses that PS1-era genre-bending weirdness.

  • Mythargia

    RunByCoffee · 2023-02-20

    Mythargia casts you as a journalist chasing a lead to an abandoned island tied to two dimensions. Instead of combat or action-driven progression, the game centers on a build-your-own-story mechanic: gathering journal entries and photos to piece together the article yourself. Reviewers praised this as genuinely original, likening it to Frogwares' investigative titles. A short, atmospheric pixel-art mystery for players who want their curiosity to do the heavy lifting.

  • Rogue Party

    Chris Antoni · 2018-10-31

    Rogue Party throws one to four rogues into the randomized Dungeon of Doom, where chests, enemies, and bosses shift every run. It's FPS-style dungeon shooting layered with roguelike loot, potions, and drop-in drop-out multiplayer. Reviewers admit it's rough around the edges but say it shines in co-op, packed with strategy, laughs, and an active developer still adding content. If you love messy, chaotic dungeon runs with friends, this small-budget crawler delivers exactly that.

  • Chronescher

    PurpleSloth · 2022-08-31

    Chronescher drops you into an Escher-esque world where geometry bends and nothing stays where you left it. Place portals, snapshot the level with time anchors, and shift perspective to uncover paths that shouldn't exist. Spread across six biomes, each introducing new mechanics, it builds toward a quiet mystery about who you're actually playing. Reviewers compare it favorably to Monument Valley and Manifold Garden, calling out the low price for the amount of content. With only 58 reviews, it's a puzzle game that hasn't found its audience yet.

  • REVISITOR

    Lead Games · 2021-11-19

    Revisitor is a first-person puzzle-platformer built by a solo developer, set inside a simulated 'Revisitor program.' You navigate with two teleport types, Return and Blink, through increasingly tricky missions filled with hidden logs and challenge objectives. Reviewers compare it favorably to Portal 2, praising its smooth movement and clever mechanics. Best of all, it's completely free, with players reporting eight to fifteen hours of content to fully complete.

  • SokoWinter

    Gray Boss · 2022-03-13

    SokoWinter takes the classic sokoban formula and adds one sharp twist: ice. Push blocks across slippery tiles where everything keeps sliding until it hits something solid, and suddenly every puzzle demands new thinking. With 100 handcrafted levels, four unlockable penguins, an undo button, and a minimalist winter aesthetic, it's the kind of low-pressure puzzle game that rewards patience without ever feeling punishing.
